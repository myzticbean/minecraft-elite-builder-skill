# How an expert builds

This is the doctrine. It is distilled from real builds (a legendary forge and the trial builds
before it), from the user's edits to them in game, and from studying how other AI building tools
work.

## Tiers

| Tier | Size | Has |
|---|---|---|
| Trial (basic) | a site of about 40 by 40 by 19, about 6,500 blocks with its ground | Two volumes with two jobs and two materials. One landmark (a chimney with a crown). A bellcast roof with stair outlines. Every side dressed differently. The trade's parts. A lantern every 4 blocks. One or two review passes. |
| Legendary | a site of about 50 by 60 by 28, about 16,000 blocks | A story of how it grew: three or so volumes, each in its own era's material. A silhouette with a clear order. A shape vocabulary (round, triangle, vertical, diagonal). Three layers of depth on every face. Curves. Gradient textures. Interiors with visible structure. Water used for its purpose. A whole site dug into a hill. Five or so review passes. |

Bigger is not the goal. Every extra block must buy a layer of depth or a detail; a big plain wall is
worse than a small plain one.

## 1. Story before blocks

- **Start with history, not an outline.** Who works there, what they make, how old the place is,
  how it grew. A building that grew over time explains its own variety.
  - The forge's three generations: the grandfather's stone smithy, now the chimney tower; the
    father's timber hall with a water wheel and trip hammer; the master's jettied house, bridged
    to the hall.
- **Each generation gets its own material and its own era of detail**: rough stone, then a heavy
  oak frame, then fine plaster and carved brackets. Repairs show.
- **Start from what the trade does, not from what a house looks like.** List the real working
  parts first. Then the building is shaped round them.
  - A smithy needs: a hearth, bellows, an anvil a step from the fire, a quench tub, a grindstone,
    a tool rack and fuel. Outside: a trough and hitching rail, a wagon, a woodpile, a slag heap and
    a sign.
  - The fire needs an open shed; the goods need a closed shop.
- **A great one has things a common one doesn't.** The legendary forge has water power, a trip
  hammer, a charcoal clamp and a farrier's stocks.
- **Name the inspirations in the docstring**: real places (Tudor jettied houses, medieval hammer
  mills) and famous RPGs (Skyrim's Warmaiden's and Skyforge, The Witcher 3's Hattori). Copy none of
  them 1:1.

## 2. Massing and silhouette first

- **Block out the volumes in plain blocks and judge them from the approach** before any texture.
  The silhouette against the sky is most of the impression.
- **Several volumes of different heights, in a clear order:**
  - one landmark, tallest (the tower or chimney);
  - one main mass (the hall's steep gable);
  - lower supporting masses (the house, the bridge);
  - small accents (a turret, a louvre, secondary stacks).
- **Two volumes, two jobs, two materials.** One volume dressed up never reads as rich as two that
  differ.
- **Asymmetric but balanced.** The forge has its tower off-centre at the back, and the round wheel
  on the other side to answer it.
- **Shape contrast**: round (a wheel), triangle (gables), vertical (a tower, a spire), diagonal (a
  flume on trestles). A row of boxes under the same roof never reads as rich.
- **Roofs differ in height and pitch, and never run into one another's slopes.**
  - Never start a lower roof on the line of a higher one's slope: it reads as one long roof.
  - Give a lower wing its own gable with eaves at its own beam. A band of the higher wall then
    shows between the roofs; dress it like any other front.
- **A tower is a low base and a tall shaft.** A base as tall as the shaft above it reads stubby.
- **Keep the hero line clear.** From the front-left corner, nothing tall should sit in front of the
  best feature.

## 3. Scale

- **Human scale:**
  - doors 2 high;
  - rooms at least 3 high;
  - windows start a block above the floor.
- **Legendary scale goes about half again bigger:**
  - storeys of 5 (a floor plus 4);
  - a hall 9 high inside;
  - spans of 13;
  - roofs about as tall as the walls under them.
- **Stone walls are two thick wherever they are stone**, so windows and arches get real reveals.
- **Things keep their real size.** A farrier's stocks are as tall as a horse: a 2-high frame with a
  rail at 3. At 4 high and 5 long they read as a gazebo.
- **Count proportions from a known small block, never by eye**: a lantern is 6/16 wide, a button
  6/16, a log end one block. On a reference image, count the infill blocks between posts.
- **Proportion is the first thing that's wrong.** Narrow and tall reads as a real house; wide under
  a low roof reads as a fat one.

## 4. Depth: three layers on every face

A flat face is the clearest sign of an amateur. Every face gets:

1. **The plane**: infill, plaster or stone face.
2. **Structure, a block proud**: posts, beams, quoins, pilasters, buttresses, string courses,
   plinths.
3. **Projections**: jetties, brackets, corbels, sills, hoods, window boxes, shutters, and roof
   overhangs with a visible underside.
4. **Recesses**: windows in a two-thick wall (the pane or bars in the inner cell, the outer cell a
   reveal), doors under arches, arrow slits.

Depth is everything stood **out** from a wall. A one-thick wall has no "in" except the room, so the
pane stays in the wall's own cell.

Rhythm matters as much as depth:

- **No plane longer than about 6 blocks** without a change of depth.
- **Detail every 4 to 6 blocks, with plain wall between**, so the detail reads. Rhythm, not
  saturation.
- **Dress every side, and dress each one differently.** No side mirrors another; move a window by
  one on purpose.
- **Draw outlines in stairs**: eave tips, gable outlines and counter faces. A stair edge reads as a
  moulding; a full block reads as a box.
- **Thin things stay thin.** An awning is carpet on top-half trapdoors. A barge board is a stair
  with one upside-down stair under it.

## 5. Curves

Straight 45° lines are the second sign of a basic build.

- **Bellcast roofs**: shallow at the eave (a slab, then stairs), steeper toward the ridge (two up
  for one across, stair over a full block). A curve is a table, not a formula.
- **Arches**: round heads on the great door, on the windows and on doors. Where the curve falls
  half-way through a block, use an upside-down stair or a top slab.
- **Corbels and brackets**: stairs stepping out under jetties, turrets and chimney crowns.
- **Round things**: a water wheel, octagonal (cut-corner) towers, domes (a charcoal clamp).
- **A spire ends in a point**: a steep pyramid, a block, a wall post and a rod. A stepped pyramid
  with a block on top reads squat.

## 6. Texture: gradients with a reason

- **Random noise is not texture.** Every mix runs on a gradient that tells a story:
  - **dirty at the foot, clean at the top**: moss and cobble at the plinth, stone bricks above,
    cleaner and lighter blocks high up;
  - **soot where fire is**: deepslate, then blackstone, darker toward the chimney's crown;
  - **weather where water runs**: moss under eaves and windows, damp stones at a race.
- **Patches, not salt**: blocks in clusters (the patch hash in `look.resolve`), never one in ten
  spread evenly.
- **Rough materials get a mix; smooth ones stay clean.**
  - Stone, brick, wood and earth take 3 or 4 close blocks.
  - Plaster, concrete and glass take one block, or a fine 9:1 grain at most.
- **Value contrast between materials** (dark roof, pale plaster, mid stone, dark timber), and **one
  accent colour per house** (the forge's is fire orange).
- **Shared theme, trade accent** across a town: every house has the same timber, stone and slate;
  each is told apart by its trade. For example:
  - a provisioner's striped stall;
  - a forge's fire;
  - an assayer's copper and bars;
  - an alchemist's purple glass and funnel.

**Styles, by their materials and signature moves:**

| Style | Materials and signature moves |
|---|---|
| Medieval | Stone bricks with cobble and mossy variants, a dark or spruce timber frame, steep spruce or dark oak roofs, deepslate tiles as slate. Battlements, arrow slits, round towers with cone roofs, banners, hanging lanterns. |
| Rustic | Oak and spruce log walls on a cobble foundation, sloped roofs, a stone chimney with a campfire. Porches, fences, hay, barrels, window boxes, a well, a winding dirt path. |
| Nordic | Dark spruce and dark oak longhouses, steep gables with carved ends, moss or hay roofs, cobble and andesite footings. Carpet as furs, fire pits, drying racks. |
| Japanese | Low and wide, curved multi-tier dark roofs with upturned eaves, white walls framed in dark wood or crimson, cherry and bamboo planks. Stone lanterns, raised walkways, gravel and pond gardens. |
| Steampunk | Copper in all its stages, iron bars, polished andesite, dark oak, gold as brass. Chimneys, pipes, round windows, catwalks. |
| Fantasy | Purpur, amethyst, prismarine, cherry, moss; shroomlight, glowstone, end rods. Twisting towers, organic shapes over boxes. |
| Modern | Concrete, quartz, glass, smooth stone. Flat roofs, cantilevers, hidden strip lights, no weathering at all. |

## 7. Micro-detail with meaning

- **Every small block says something about use.** Build a vocabulary per trade
  (`vocabulary.md` has the forge's).
- **Light everywhere it would be**: under each beam end, over the anvil, at the sign, on the hoist,
  in the loft.
- **A story in the props**: smoke, firewood, a cart with a wheel off, barrels you can read from the
  street, flower boxes, signs.
- **Less clutter on working surfaces.** A counter with two things on it reads better
  than one covered in clutter.

## 8. Interior is part of the build

- **Visible structure**: trusses, tie beams on stone corbels, a king post, rafters open over the
  top room.
- **Zones by use**: hot work, cold work, storage, the master's gallery.
- **Props in working order**: the anvil a step from the fire, the quench tub beside it, fuel by the
  hearth.
- **Light from the work itself.**
- **Circulation**: the path, then the entrance, the rooms and every storey. Use continuous stairs
  or ladders with 2 blocks of headroom; `look.check` proves each floor is reached.

## 9. Ground and setting

- **The building grows out of the slope.**
  - Dig into the hill at the back, with stone retaining walls where the cut meets the hill.
  - The plinth steps with the ground.
  - The hill is drawn after the buildings, into empty cells only.
- **Paths worn on the routes people walk**: cobble at the door, then gravel, dirt and grass, with
  ragged edges.
- **Round the base**: coarse dirt, podzol, gravel and packed mud. Never a clean grass carpet.
- **A hill tapers.** It falls away at both ends and at the back, so the site's edge shows no cliff.
  Put rock only on real steps (three or more) and in two-by-two outcrops, or the slope looks
  salted.
- **Clutter thins out from the work zones.** Trees and bushes go in mixed groups; greenery at the
  feet of walls.
- **Water used for its purpose**: a race to the wheel, then a pond. Imply the flow with the wheel
  and a sluice board; never pour a real waterfall.

## 10. Light, and night

The money shot is at night: fire through the great arch, smoke against the dark.

- Lanterns where people walk, on brackets and chains, every 4 to 6 blocks.
- Fire light where the work is: lava in a closed bed, magma, campfires.
- Hidden `light` blocks only where a dark corner would breed mobs, and never under a slab.

## 11. Review like a builder

- **Judge from the player's road first** (the hero corner, front left), then the other corners, then
  close views cropped to each volume. Site-wide renders are too small to judge.
- **Ask, every pass**:
  - Does the silhouette have an order?
  - Does any roof run into another?
  - Is any face flat?
  - Is any plane longer than 6 without depth?
  - Are two sides the same?
  - Does anything stand at the wrong scale?
  - Does anything block the hero line?
  - Is there a cliff at the site's edge?
  - Does any wall have no light?
- **Fix concrete defects, then stop.** Don't restart a sound design or add unrelated embellishment
  to use up a pass.
- **Exact cells beat perspective.** Settle a render's doubt with `review.py dump` before changing
  anything.
